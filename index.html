Learning Design & Technology Reflection

A synthesis of background, goals, and core takeaways

Background & Voice

1. Background

My journey in higher education administration began in a college art gallery as Associate Team Leader for Educational Programs, where I developed K-6 gallery programming. Since then, I’ve supported virtual programs for Smithsonian Associates, managed enrollment and student wellbeing as a Program Coordinator for two graduate programs at Georgetown, and currently help launch the Emerging Studies Institute as an incubator for new university initiatives.

Background & Voice

2. Unique Contributions

I bring rich hands-on administrative experience running higher ed programs and events within resource-constrained environments. My background bridges direct student support, admissions operations, and community arts facilitation, offering practical insight into how institutional workflows impact the student experience.

Program Intent & Vision

3. Program Intent

I joined LDT to complement my practical operational experience with formal tenets of program design and learning theory. I want to build a deeper understanding of the broader higher education landscape and gain practical experience working directly with external clients.

Program Intent & Vision

4. Current Curiosities

I am deeply curious about how creative and artistic education—like informal poetry workshops—fosters self-confidence, personal expression, and critical thinking. I am eager to explore how these creative, community-based learning frameworks can be translated into formal higher education models.

Program Intent & Vision

5. Open Questions

How can institutional program design balance operational efficiency with authentic, human-centered student support? How do we ensure that administrative goals remain strictly aligned with true student learning outcomes rather than procedural convenience?

Program Intent & Vision

6. Long-Term Vision

Beyond LDT, I aim to specialize within higher education—exploring roles in student support services, program management, or institutional communications. Additionally, I intend to apply these learning design principles to empower independent creative learning communities outside of academia.

Foundations & Reflection

7. Core Takeaways from Foundations

  • Backward Design: Learning to start with the desired learning outcomes first, addressing the oft-ignored question of what a program should actually accomplish before building its structure.
  • Measurable Success: Shifting the focus from simple program execution to establishing concrete metrics that evaluate student growth and program effectiveness.
  • Student-Centered Alignment: Understanding how to re-center the user/student experience to prevent projects from drifting away from their primary educational goals.
Foundations & Reflection

8. Scenario Impact Analysis

Engaging with practical design scenarios highlighted how frequently institutional projects aim for one goal but yield unintended outcomes due to front-end design gaps. Experiencing this through Foundations shifted my perspective from viewing program management as merely logistical execution to seeing it as an intentional, outcome-driven learning process centered entirely on student success.
